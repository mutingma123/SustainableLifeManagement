\section{Test Instances and Data Design}
Three instances are used in the computational study.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Small instance}: 5 tasks, 3 agents, and 3 scenarios.
    \item \textbf{Medium instance}: 10 tasks, 5 agents, and 5 scenarios.
    \item \textbf{Large instance}: 25 tasks, 10 agents, and 10 scenarios.
\end{itemize}

\section{Computational Results}
\subsection{Overall summary}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Computational Results Summary}
\label{tab:results_summary}
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
Instance & Final objective & Total return & $Z$ & Iterations & Cuts added & Final Benders gap \\
\midrule
Small  & 51.4982  & 53.0000  & 0.3755 & 2   & 1    & 0.0000 \\
Medium & 77.1139  & 79.0000  & 0.3772 & 52  & 220  & 0.0000 \\
Large  & 254.3369 & 257.0000 & 0.4438 & 443 & 1958 & 0.0000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The results show a clear computational pattern. The small instance is solved after only two iterations and one feasibility cut. The medium instance is already much more demanding, requiring 52 iterations and 220 cuts. The large instance is the most expensive case, requiring 443 iterations and 1958 cuts. This confirms that the number of Benders iterations and the number of feasibility cuts increase substantially with instance size.

\subsection{Small instance}
The first master-optimal solution is infeasible because agent 1 is assigned an unsafe bundle. One feasibility cut excludes the offending subset, and the second iteration produces a feasible and optimal solution.

The final assignment groups tasks 1 and 3 with agent 3, tasks 2 and 4 with agent 2, and task 5 with agent 1. The workload imbalance is not negligible, but the solution satisfies the chance constraints and remains optimal with respect to the original objective.

\subsection{Medium instance}
The final solution does not assign all tasks. Tasks 1, 6, 8, and 9 are left unassigned, which indicates that under the current return, workload, and overload-risk settings, it is better to leave some tasks undone than to violate the chance constraints or create excessive imbalance.

\subsection{Large instance}
The final solution leaves six tasks unassigned, namely tasks 5, 11, 12, 17, 23, and 24. Most agents receive two tasks, but agent 7 receives only one task. The workload pattern is therefore feasible but not perfectly balanced.
